> Feeling someone else's pain does nothing to alleviate their suffering. It merely spreads that pain to another individual.
>
> When you visit a sick friend, or encounter someone who is terminally ill, they rarely have to ask for your pity. They don't need to. Instead, they make subtle displays of their suffering, showing just how terrible they feel and how difficult things have become. Even the slightest movement can convey how terrifying the prospect of death is. Consciously or unconsciously, the sick person does this.
> 
> This is an expression of the will to power that underlies many human actions. We are often secretly, and sometimes quite openly, driven by the pursuit of power. Even someone who is completely decrepit and powerless, someone who has lost almost every form of agency, still retains the power to inflict pain on others.
> 
> And this is precisely what pity does: it gives the suffering person the power to make others suffer alongside them.
>
> ~ The Nietzsche Podcast, Episode 3
