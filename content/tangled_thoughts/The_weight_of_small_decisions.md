title: 'The Weight of Small Decisions'
date: 2026-11-10 20:00:00
tags: ["Random"]
---

It's just so fucking hard to stick to a routine. Because it's not like you have to make the right decision just once. No, you have to make the right decision every second of the rest of your life. And so much can go wrong so quickly.

It's winter. The room is cold. It's so hard to get out of bed when you could just lie there a little longer. And it's so easy to tell yourself that this decision doesn't matter, that it doesn't really matter whether you stay in bed for another few minutes or not. But then you do, and you miss the next bus or train, so now you're late.

You tell yourself that even if something goes wrong, you know what's important, and you will keep going regardless. You will meditate anyway, drink your morning cup of coffee, go for a run, or whatever it is you had planned. But then you're late, and you think: I'm already so late. I can't waste my time on these less important things. I have to get to university. So you skip them.

Then you come home, exhausted from a long day of thinking and working. You make some simple food and sit down in front of the television or YouTube, where an infinite stream of passive entertainment is waiting for you. You don't have to do anything. The next click, the next video, is so easy. The process is effortless. It's so hard to steer your thoughts towards something else.

You didn't actually want to spend all your time passively consuming things. You wanted to be active, to create something, to do something. But it's so difficult when the next click, the next little dose of mindless enjoyment, is just one click away. All it takes is a slight movement of your mouse.

And why would you get up and go outside to exercise? Your room is warm and cozy, while outside it's dark and cold. And how much effort does it take to write or draw something? You've been thinking all day. You don't want to think anymore. You don't want to reach for words, look things up, or struggle to put your thoughts into something tangible.

Before you know it, hours have passed.

You know you need to go to bed if you want to wake up early enough the next morning. But the next video is right there. What are a few more minutes? You'll just sleep a little less, or wake up a little later. So you click. And click again. One video after another.

It's past midnight, and you know you've fucked up. You go to bed, wake up tired, and tell yourself: What are a few more minutes in bed?

And so everything repeats.

And the worst part is that you know exactly what is happening. You can see the pattern repeating itself. You know that those few minutes in bed, that one additional video, that decision to skip meditation, are all part of the same thing. And yet, knowing all of this doesn't seem to change anything.

Trapped in a prison of your own making.
